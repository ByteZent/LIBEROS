> [!bluf]
> Every note is **atomic** (one idea), **sourced** (claims trace back to references), **linked** (placed in the network) and **rated** (maturity and confidence), so readers know how much weight each note can carry.

## Principles

1. **Atomicity.** One note, one idea. If a note needs two BLUFs, it is two notes.
2. **Own words.** Definitions are quoted *and* paraphrased. Paraphrase is the test of understanding.
3. **Links carry meaning.** A link says *how* two notes relate: supports, contradicts, is an instance of, operationalises.
4. **Primary before secondary.** Read the original (Clausewitz, the doctrine, the law) before the commentary.
5. **Steelman the critique.** Every concept note has a *Critiques & Limits* section.
6. **Separate fact from judgement.** Judgements live in `[!assessment]` callouts and carry a confidence level.

## Maturity

| Marker | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Seedling** | Captured and structured, but thin. Definitions and first links. |
| **Developing** | Sourced, critiqued, linked to cases. Still being revised. |
| **Evergreen** | Stable and well-sourced. I would defend it in a seminar. |

## Confidence

Applies to the note's analytic claims, not to the facts it reports.

| Level | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **High** | Multiple independent, authoritative sources; broad scholarly consensus. |
| **Medium** | Well-supported but contested, or based on a limited source base. |
| **Low** | Tentative interpretation, single source, or my own speculation. |

## Source ratings

For assessments of current events, sources are graded on the NATO/Admiralty system: **reliability A–F** (source track record) × **credibility 1–6** (confirmation of the information).

## Borders on the map

The world map of developments uses the borders of Natural Earth (public domain), which draws a territory with the state that controls it. That is a description of control, not my position on who it belongs to. Breakaway and disputed areas (Crimea, the occupied parts of Georgia and Ukraine, Northern Cyprus, Western Sahara, Kashmir, the Golan Heights and others) are therefore hatched and take no country's shade. A development is placed where it happened; `location:` names the state under international law (`UKR` for an event in Crimea).
