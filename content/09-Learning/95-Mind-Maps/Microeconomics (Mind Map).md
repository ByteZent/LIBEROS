> [!bluf]
> The course Microeconomics on one page, in the order in which one idea builds on the next: first the way of thinking (scarcity, opportunity cost, the margin, incentives), then the two first models, trade, the market, how strongly a market reacts, and the household behind the demand curve. The four branches after that are the rest of the semester: efficiency and interventions, firms, market power and market failure. They are outlines until their notes exist.
>
> Each leaf is a note of the vault, an idea in a few words, an open question taken from a note, or a gap. Filter by `T1`, `T2` or `T3` to see what belongs to which test, by `todo` for the notes that are still missing, and by `question` for what I cannot answer yet.

> - Thinking like an economist `T1`
>     - [[Ten Principles of Economics]]
>         - Scarcity: every choice has an opportunity cost `idea`
>         - Decisions are made at the margin, not on the average `idea`
>         - People respond to incentives `idea`
>         - Markets usually allocate well, but they can fail `idea`
>         - A shown market failure is not yet a case for intervention `idea`
>     - Positive says what is, normative says what should be `idea`
>     - Answer scheme: assumptions, mechanism, result, interpretation, limit `idea`
> - The first two models `T1`
>     - [[Circular Flow Diagram]]
>         - Two actors, two markets: households and firms, goods and factors `idea`
>         - Every real flow is matched by an opposite money flow `idea`
>         - One actor's spending is another actor's income `idea`
>         - Which households' incomes rise first when defence procurement surges? `question`
>     - [[Production Possibilities Frontier]]
>         - Inside is inefficient, on the curve efficient, outside unattainable `idea`
>         - The slope is the opportunity cost `idea`
>         - Bowed outward: producing more costs more at the margin `idea`
>         - Growth shifts the frontier, a choice moves along it `idea`
>         - How fast can an economy move from civilian to defence production? `question`
>     - The circular flow shows who trades with whom, the frontier what can be produced `idea`
> - Trade `T1`
>     - [[Comparative Advantage]]
>         - Absolute advantage: fewer inputs. Comparative advantage: lower opportunity cost `idea`
>         - Trade pays even for the one who is better at everything `idea`
>         - Both gain if the price lies between the two opportunity costs `idea`
>         - The country gains, but not every person in it `idea`
>         - What does Switzerland pay for security of supply? `question`
>         - When does interdependence prevent conflict, and when is it a weapon? `question`
> - The market `T1`
>     - [[Supply and Demand]]
>         - [[Supply and Demand#Demand|Demand]]: falls when the price rises
>         - [[Supply and Demand#Supply|Supply]]: rises when the price rises
>         - [[Supply and Demand#Equilibrium|Equilibrium]]: a surplus pushes the price down, a shortage pushes it up
>         - The good's own price moves along a curve, anything else shifts it `idea`
>         - Three steps: which curve, which direction, new equilibrium `idea`
>         - Double shifts: one of the two effects is ambiguous `idea`
>         - A higher price alone does not show that demand rose `idea`
>         - Prices do two jobs: they signal and they ration `idea`
>         - How steep is short-run supply in the defence industries? `question`
>         - Prices as signals in a disaster, or exploitation? Who draws the line? `question`
> - How strongly a market reacts `T1`
>     - [[Elasticity]]
>         - Supply and demand give the direction, elasticity gives the size `idea`
>         - A ratio of two percentage changes, so it has no unit `idea`
>         - The four elasticities
>             - Price elasticity of demand
>             - Price elasticity of supply
>             - Income elasticity: normal or inferior good
>             - Cross-price elasticity: substitutes or complements
>         - The number to remember is 1: elastic above, inelastic below `idea`
>         - Midpoint method for computing it `idea`
>         - [[Elasticity#Elasticity and total revenue|Total revenue]]: a price rise lowers it where demand is elastic `idea`
>         - Slope is not elasticity: a straight demand curve has many `idea`
>         - More elastic with close substitutes, a narrow market and time `idea`
>         - How many years until the supply of ammunition becomes elastic? `question`
>         - Which Swiss imports have the most inelastic demand? `question`
> - Behind the demand curve `T1`
>     - [[Consumer Choice]]
>         - Budget constraint: what she can afford `idea`
>         - Indifference curves: what she wants `idea`
>         - Optimum: marginal rate of substitution equals relative price `idea`
>         - A price change works twice: substitution effect and income effect `idea`
>         - Giffen good and falling hours of work: the two effects pull against each other `idea`
>         - The demand curve is derived from these optimal choices `idea`
>         - Is service pay a wage, or compensation for a duty? `question`
>         - Rationing and price ceilings: who loses most? `question`
>     - How real choices deviate from the optimum: [[Heuristics and Anchoring]]

## How to use this map

- One line per node, a few words each. Indent with four spaces to go one level deeper.
- Link freely: `[[Note]]`, `[[Note#Heading]]`, `[[/flashcards/|a page of the site]]`, `[text](https://…)`.
- A word in backticks is a tag and becomes a filter: `idea`, `question`, `source`, `todo`, or a test block: `T1`, `T2`, `T3`.
- A `todo` branch is a part of the course that has no note yet. Its leaves are the headings to expect, not what was taught.
- When a branch has grown into something I understand, write the note and replace the branch by a link to it.
