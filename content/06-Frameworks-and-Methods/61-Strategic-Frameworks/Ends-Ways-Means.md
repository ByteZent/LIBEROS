> [!bluf]
> Arthur Lykke's formula, **Strategy = Ends + Ways + Means**, is the standard planning heuristic taught at Western war colleges. It checks that objectives, methods and resources are coherent, and that the gap between them (the **risk**) is acknowledged. It is a useful *checklist* but a poor *theory*: it can make strategy look like arithmetic rather than creative problem-solving against a thinking adversary.

## Purpose

To test whether a strategy is coherent and feasible, and to make risk explicit.

## Components

1. **Ends:** objectives. What political outcome is sought?
2. **Ways:** concepts. How will resources be used (e.g. attrition, manoeuvre, [[Deterrence]], coercion)?
3. **Means:** resources. Forces, money, time, political capital.
4. **Risk:** the imbalance between the three. Lykke's image is a **three-legged stool** that tips when one leg is too short.

## Worked Example

Apply the model to a national defence strategy: are the stated *ends* achievable with the budgeted *means* through the chosen *ways*? Where is the gap, and who accepts the risk?

> [!counter] Critique
> - **Meiser (2016/17):** the formula encourages "means-based" planning and neglects the *ways*, which is where the real creative strategy lies.
> - It is static and one-sided: the adversary also adapts, and strategy is interactive (see [[Carl von Clausewitz|Clausewitz]]).
> - It treats ends as given, whereas in reality ends are often revised as means and ways reveal what is possible.
