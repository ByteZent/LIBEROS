> [!bluf]
> The mind has **bottlenecks** where things can no longer be done in parallel. **Attention** selects what gets through. The price is that people miss what they are not attending to, even when it is in plain view (**inattentional blindness**), and that doing two demanding tasks at once makes both slower.
>
> The way out is **automatisation**: with enough practice a task needs little or no thought, which frees attention for something else. This is the psychological case for **drill**. The cost: an automated process is hard to stop when it is the wrong one (**Stroop effect**).

## Definition

**Attention** selects the information to be processed at the bottlenecks where parallel processing is no longer possible.

| Kind | What it does | Phenomenon |
|---|---|---|
| **Auditory attention** | Can strengthen or weaken what is heard | Cocktail-party effect: following one voice in a noisy room |
| **Visual attention** | Can be focused on a few degrees of the visual field, and the focus can be moved to take in a complex scene | Inattentional blindness |
| **Divided attention** | Trying to run two tasks at once | Marked slowing |

## Key Points

- **Inattentional blindness.** People fail to see a clearly visible object or event because their attention is on something else [@simons1999gorillas]. In the "door" study many people giving directions to a stranger did not notice that the stranger was swapped for another person while a door was carried between them [@simons1998failure].
- **Divided attention is costly.** In an experiment people saw three digits and had to (1) check whether the third was the sum of the first two and (2) give the product of the first and third. Doing both in parallel took much longer than doing them one after the other. **Mostly, people can follow only one train of thought at a time.**
- **Automatisation.** Practice reduces the central cognitive part of a task. With heavy training a task needs little or no thinking effort. Examples: driving a car, weapon handling drills.
- **Stroop effect.** Asked to name the ink colour of a colour word printed in a different colour, people are slow and make errors, because reading is automatic and cannot be switched off [@stroop1935studies]. **Automated processes are hard to suppress.**
- **Limited resources.** The cognitive system has a fixed capacity. Under stress it shrinks further.

## Application

- **Why drill.** A soldier who must think about how to clear a stoppage has no attention left for the enemy. Drilled to automaticity, the hands do the work and the head stays free for the situation. Drill is not the opposite of thinking: it is what makes thinking under fire possible. **This is why drill is a necessary element in the development of our soldiers.**
- **What to drill, and what not.** Automate what is the same every time (weapon handling, first aid steps, radio procedure, immediate action). Do not automate judgement. An automated response fires even when the situation has changed, as Stroop shows.
- **Sentry duty and observation.** Give an observer one task and one sector. An observer who also has to operate a radio and keep a log will miss things in plain view.
- **Briefings and orders.** People listening while doing something else take in little. Stop the activity, then give the order.
- **Screens and multitasking.** A commander or driver who divides attention between a display and the surroundings is slower at both. Staff procedures should hand tasks over, not stack them on one person. **This is also the reasoning behind the maxim "Wer führt, fährt nicht": leaders should not occupy themselves with driving. They should concentrate on leading.**
- **Deception.** Draw the enemy's attention to one place so that he fails to see what happens in another. Inattentional blindness is the mechanism behind every feint.

> [!counter] Critiques & Limits
> - **Drill can make units rigid.** Units that are drilled without understanding apply the drill where it does not fit. Drill needs to be paired with training in recognising when the situation is different.
> - **Laboratory tasks are narrow.** The size of dual-task costs depends on how similar the tasks are. Walking and talking combine well. Two tasks that both need words or both need the eyes do not.
> - **Experts chunk.** Experienced people handle more at once, not because they have more attention, but because they have automated more and see patterns where novices see single items.
> - **"Shorter attention spans" of the young** are often claimed (see [[Generations and the Military]]) and poorly measured.

## Key Connections

- [[Perception]]: attention decides which stimuli are perceived at all.
- [[Memory]]: automated skills are procedural memory. What is not attended to is not stored.
- [[Heuristics and Anchoring]]: because capacity is limited, the mind uses shortcuts.
- [[OODA Loop]]: drill shortens the loop by removing the conscious decision for standard cases. Boyd calls this implicit guidance and control.
- [[Attrition vs Manoeuvre Warfare]]: manoeuvre aims at overloading the enemy's attention with more events than he can process.
- [[Cognitive Warfare]]: attention is the scarce resource that it fights for.

## Self-Test

> [!question]- 1. What does attention do?
> It selects the information to be processed at the bottlenecks where things can no longer be done in parallel.

> [!question]- 2. What is inattentional blindness? Give an example.
> Failing to see a clearly visible object or event because attention is elsewhere. Example: not noticing that the person you are talking to has been swapped behind a passing door.

> [!question]- 3. What did the three-digit experiment show?
> Doing two thinking tasks in parallel takes much longer. People can mostly follow only one train of thought at a time.

> [!question]- 4. What does automatisation achieve, and how?
> Through practice the central cognitive part of a task shrinks, so that it needs little or no thinking effort and attention is free for other things.

> [!question]- 5. What does the Stroop effect show?
> Automated processes (here: reading) are hard to suppress, even when they interfere with the task.

> [!question]- 6. Apply this: why is weapon handling drilled?
> So that it runs automatically and the soldier's limited attention stays free for the situation and the enemy.
