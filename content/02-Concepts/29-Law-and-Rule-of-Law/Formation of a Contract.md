> [!bluf]
> A contract comes into being through **matching mutual declarations of intent** (Art. 1 OR): an **offer** and an **acceptance**. The declarations can be express or implied. No form is needed unless a statute requires one.
>
> Three things decide most cases: the offer **binds** the offeror, the binding **lasts only for a limited time**, and **silence is normally a refusal**.

## Definition

**Art. 1 OR:** the conclusion of a contract requires a mutual expression of intent by the parties that matches. It may be express or implied.

| | Offer (*Antrag, Offerte*) | Acceptance (*Annahme, Akzept*) |
|---|---|---|
| **Order** | First declaration in time | Second declaration in time |
| **Content** | The will to conclude a contract with a definite content, so that only the other side's "yes" is still needed | The final will to conclude, by agreeing to the contract as offered |

In my own words: an offer is complete enough that the other side can close the deal with a single "yes". Anything that still needs negotiating is not yet an offer.

## Key Points

- **The offer binds.** It cannot be revoked or changed by the offeror alone. The recipient has the power to bring the contract into being by accepting, without any further act of the offeror.
- **How long the offer binds:**

    | Situation | Norm | Binding lasts |
    |---|---|---|
    | Offer **with** a time limit | Art. 3 OR | Until the limit expires |
    | Offer **without** time limit, **between persons present** (also by telephone) | Art. 4 OR | Acceptance must follow immediately |
    | Offer **without** time limit, **between absent persons** | Art. 5 OR | Until an answer can be expected if it is sent properly and in time |

- **Silence is refusal, as a rule.** Exception (Art. 6 OR): silence counts as acceptance where an express acceptance is not to be expected
    - because of the **special nature of the transaction** (for example a gift), or
    - because of the **circumstances** (for example negotiations already under way, or an existing framework contract).
- **Unsolicited goods are not an offer** (Art. 6a OR). The recipient need not return or keep them. If they were obviously sent by mistake, the recipient must tell the sender.
- **Freedom of form** (Art. 11 OR). Contracts are valid without any special form unless the law prescribes one. Oral and electronic contracts are therefore valid in principle. See [[Validity of a Contract]].
- **Good faith** (Art. 2 ZGB) governs how declarations are read: everyone must act in good faith, and the obvious abuse of a right is not protected. A declaration means what a reasonable recipient could understand by it.

## Application

**Checking whether a contract was formed:**
1. Is there an **offer**: definite enough and made with the will to be bound?
2. Was the offer **still binding** when the answer came (Art. 3–5 OR)?
3. Is there an **acceptance** that matches the offer? An "acceptance" with changes is a new offer.
4. If nobody answered: does an exception for **silence** apply (Art. 6 OR)?
5. Then check validity: [[Validity of a Contract]].

**Typical traps:**
- A late acceptance does not create a contract. It can be treated as a new offer.
- Formation and validity are separate questions. A contract can be formed and still be void or voidable.
- The burden of proof lies with whoever derives rights from the alleged fact (Art. 8 ZGB). An oral contract is valid but hard to prove.

> [!counter] Critiques & Limits
> - **The offer-acceptance model fits simple exchanges.** Long negotiations, where the parties move towards each other step by step, are hard to split into one offer and one acceptance.
> - **Standard terms and online contracts.** One click "accepts" pages that nobody reads. The model treats this as consent.
> - **Protection of the recipient versus freedom of the offeror.** Binding the offeror protects the other side's planning. Other legal systems let an offer be withdrawn until it is accepted.

## Self-Test: Contract Formation

> [!qard]- 1. What does Art. 1 OR require for a contract?
> Matching mutual declarations of intent, express or implied.

> [!qard]- 2. How long is an offer without a time limit binding?
> Between persons present: it must be accepted immediately (Art. 4 OR). Between absent persons: until an answer can be expected with proper and timely dispatch (Art. 5 OR).

> [!qard]- 3. Someone sends you a book you did not order, with an invoice. Do you have to pay or return it?
> No. Unsolicited goods are not an offer (Art. 6a OR), and you need neither return nor keep them. Only if they were obviously sent by mistake must you inform the sender.

> [!qard]- 4. When does silence count as acceptance?
> When an express acceptance is not to be expected because of the special nature of the transaction or the circumstances, and the offer is not refused within a reasonable time (Art. 6 OR).

> [!qard]- 5. Is an oral contract valid?
> Yes, unless the law prescribes a form (Art. 11 OR). Proof is a separate problem.

> [!qard]- 6. Apply: A makes an offer by e-mail, valid until Friday. On Monday B answers: "I accept, but at 10 % less." Is there a contract?
> No, for two reasons. The offer stopped binding on Friday (Art. 3 OR). And an acceptance with changes is not an acceptance but a new offer, which A is free to accept or refuse.

> [!qard]- 7. Compare: the formation and the validity of a contract.
> Formation asks whether matching declarations exist: offer and acceptance (Art. 1 OR). Validity asks whether the contract then holds: capacity, form, lawful content, no defect of consent. A contract can be formed and still be void.
