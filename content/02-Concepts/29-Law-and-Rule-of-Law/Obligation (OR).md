> [!bluf]
> An **obligation** is a legal relationship between two or more persons in which one (the **debtor**) owes a performance to the other (the **creditor**). Swiss law knows **four sources** of obligations: **contract**, **tort**, **unjust enrichment** and **agency without authority**.
>
> Every private-law case starts here: before asking what someone owes, ask **from which source** the obligation could arise. The source decides which conditions have to be checked.

## Definition

**Obligation:** a legal relationship between two or more persons by which one person (*Schuldner*) is bound to render a performance to the other (*Gläubiger*).

In my own words: an obligation is a legal tie between specific persons. It gives the creditor a claim against the debtor and against nobody else. That separates it from property rights, which hold against everyone.

## Key Points

### The four sources

| Source | Norm | Arises from | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Contract** (*Vertrag*) | Art. 1 ff. OR | Matching declarations of intent | Sale, lease, contract for work |
| **Tort** (*unerlaubte Handlung*) | Art. 41 ff. OR | Unlawfully causing damage to another | Damaging someone's car |
| **Unjust enrichment** (*ungerechtfertigte Bereicherung*) | Art. 62 ff. OR | Gaining at another's expense without valid ground | Payment made twice by mistake |
| **Agency without authority** (*Geschäftsführung ohne Auftrag*) | Art. 419 ff. OR | Acting in another's affairs without a mandate | Having a neighbour's burst pipe repaired while he is away |

- Contract is the only source that needs the **will of both parties**. The other three arise **by law**, whether the parties want it or not.

### Where the obligation sits in the legal order

| | Public law | Private law |
|---|---|---|
| **Relation** | State to individual, with superior authority | Between equals |
| **Typical instrument** | Decree (*Verfügung*) | Contract, tort |
| **Conditions for state action** | Legal basis, public interest, proportionality | Freedom of contract within the law |
| **Sources** | Federal Constitution, Criminal Code, Road Traffic Act, ordinances | Civil Code (ZGB) and Code of Obligations (OR), ordinances |

- **Civil Code (ZGB):** law of persons, family law, law of succession, property law. The **Code of Obligations** is formally its fifth part.
- **Code of Obligations (OR), five divisions:**
    1. General provisions
    2. The individual types of contract
    3. Commercial companies and the cooperative
    4. Commercial register, business names, accounting
    5. Securities
- **General before special.** The general provisions (for example Art. 97 OR on non-performance) apply to every contract unless the rules on the individual contract type say something different.

## Application

- **First step in any case.** Sort the facts: is there a contract? If not, was damage caused unlawfully? Was someone enriched without ground? This is step 2 of the [[Construction of a Legal Case]].
- **Several sources can apply together.** A surgeon who makes a mistake breaches the contract and commits a tort. The claimant can rely on both.
- **For officers.** Damage caused in service is mostly a matter of public liability law, not of Art. 41 OR. The private-law scheme still teaches the questions to ask: damage, unlawfulness, causation, fault.

> [!counter] Critiques & Limits
> - **The four sources are not a closed list in practice.** Courts recognise further grounds, such as liability for negotiations conducted in bad faith (*culpa in contrahendo*) and liability based on trust.
> - **Public and private law overlap.** The state also concludes ordinary contracts, and private relations are shaped by mandatory public rules (tenancy, employment).
> - **Common law systems** divide the field differently. The four-source scheme belongs to the civil-law tradition.

## Self-Test: Obligation

> [!qard]- 1. Define an obligation.
> A legal relationship between two or more persons in which the debtor owes the creditor a performance.

> [!qard]- 2. Name the four sources with their articles.
> Contract (Art. 1 ff. OR), tort (Art. 41 ff.), unjust enrichment (Art. 62 ff.), agency without authority (Art. 419 ff.).

> [!qard]- 3. Which of the four needs the will of both parties?
> Only the contract. The other three arise by law.

> [!qard]- 4. Which three conditions must state action in public law meet?
> A legal basis, a public interest and proportionality.

> [!qard]- 5. Name the five divisions of the OR.
> - General provisions
> - Individual contracts
> - Companies and cooperative
> - commercial register, business names
> - accounting; securities

> [!qard]- 6. Apply: you pay the same invoice twice by mistake. From which source does your claim for repayment arise?
> Unjust enrichment (Art. 62 ff. OR): the recipient gained at your expense without a valid ground. The contract justified only one payment. The claim arises by law, not from the will of the parties.

> [!qard]- 7. Compare: an obligation and a property right.
> An obligation gives the creditor a claim against one specific person, the debtor, and against nobody else. A property right holds against everyone.
