> [!bluf]
> In a **contract for work** (*Werkvertrag*, Art. 363 OR) the contractor owes a **result**: a finished work. The customer owes payment. This separates it from the **mandate** (*Auftrag*), where only careful activity is owed, and from the **employment contract**, where work is owed for a time under the employer's direction.
>
> If the work is defective, the customer has three rights: **refuse** it, **reduce** the price, or demand **repair** free of charge, plus damages if the contractor is at fault. These rights are lost if the customer does not **inspect and give notice** in time, and they **expire** after two or five years.

## Definition

| Contract | Norm | What is owed | Typical case |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Contract for work** (*Werkvertrag*) | Art. 363 OR | Production of a work: a **result** | Builder, tailor, software developed to specification |
| **Mandate** (*Auftrag*) | Art. 394 OR | Carrying out a business or service as agreed: **careful activity** | Doctor, lawyer |
| **Employment contract** (*Arbeitsvertrag*) | Art. 319 OR | **Work** for a fixed or indefinite time in the service of the employer, against a wage | Employee |

In my own words: ask what the customer pays for. For a finished thing that must be free of defects: contract for work. For effort and care, with no promise of success: mandate. For time spent under instructions: employment.

## Key Points

### General rule behind all of them

**Art. 97 OR:** if an obligation is not performed at all or not properly, the debtor must compensate the resulting damage, unless he proves that no fault at all lies with him. Fault is **presumed**. The special rules on the individual contracts come on top of this.

### Defect rights in the contract for work (Art. 368 OR)

| Situation | Right of the customer |
|---|---|
| Defects so serious that the work is **unusable** or acceptance cannot fairly be expected | **Refuse acceptance** (rescission, *Wandelung*) |
| **Less serious** defects | **Reduce the price** by the lower value (*Minderung*), or demand **repair free of charge** (*Nachbesserung*) if this does not cause the contractor excessive cost |
| In both cases, if the contractor is **at fault** | **Damages** in addition |

- **Works on the customer's land** that could be removed only with disproportionate harm: only reduction and repair are available, not refusal (Art. 368 Abs. 3 OR).

### Duties of the customer: inspect and give notice

1. **Inspect.** After delivery the customer must check the work as soon as the ordinary course of business allows, and tell the contractor of defects (Art. 367 OR).
2. **Approval.** If the customer approves the work expressly or tacitly, the contractor is released from liability. **Tacit approval** is assumed when the customer omits the inspection and notice (Art. 370 OR).
3. **Exceptions.** Liability remains for defects that could not be found by proper inspection (**hidden defects**) and for defects the contractor intentionally concealed.
4. **Hidden defects** must be notified once they are discovered, without delay. Otherwise the work counts as approved for them too.
5. **Buildings since 1 January 2026.** For building defects the notice period is **60 days**, for obvious defects from delivery and for hidden ones from discovery. It cannot be shortened by contract.

### Limitation periods (Art. 371 OR)

| Work | Period from acceptance |
|---|---|
| **Movable** work | 2 years |
| Movable work **built into an immovable** work and causing its defect | 5 years |
| **Immovable** work (also claims against architect and engineer) | 5 years |

## Application

**Checking a defect case:**
1. Which contract type? Is a result owed?
2. Is there a **defect**: does the work deviate from what was agreed?
3. Did the customer **inspect** and **give notice** in time? If not: approved, rights lost.
4. Which right fits: refusal, reduction or repair? Is the work on the customer's land?
5. Is the contractor at **fault**? Then damages as well.
6. Has the **limitation period** run out?

**For officers and administrators.** Procurement of equipment, buildings and software by the state uses the same private-law contracts. The lesson is practical: take delivery formally, test at once, give notice of defects in writing and keep the dates.

> [!counter] Critiques & Limits
> - **Short notice periods are harsh.** A lay customer loses every right by reacting a few days late. This is the main reason for the reform of building-defect law.
> - **The law has moved.** A revision for building defects entered into force on 1 January 2026. The notice period for building defects is now 60 days and cannot be shortened, and the right to repair can no longer be excluded by contract. The new rules also cover the sale of new buildings. The lecture slides may still show the older text of Art. 367 and 370 OR.
> - **Mixed contracts.** Many real contracts combine elements: an architect plans (work) and supervises (mandate). The classification then decides which rules apply, and it is often disputed.
> - **Contracts override the statute.** Most of these rules can be changed by agreement. In construction the SIA norm 118 usually replaces them.

## Self-Test: Werkvertrag

> [!qard]- 1. What separates a contract for work from a mandate?
> The contractor owes a result, the agent only careful activity.

> [!qard]- 2. Name the three defect rights of the customer.
> Refusal of the work (rescission), reduction of the price, repair free of charge. With fault of the contractor, damages as well.

> [!qard]- 3. What must the customer do after delivery, and what happens otherwise?
> Inspect the work as soon as feasible and notify defects. Otherwise the work counts as tacitly approved and the contractor is released.

> [!qard]- 4. For which defects does the contractor stay liable despite approval?
> For defects that could not be found by proper inspection and for defects intentionally concealed.

> [!qard]- 5. What are the limitation periods?
> Two years for movable works, five years for immovable works and for movable works built into an immovable work that cause its defect.

> [!qard]- 6. What does Art. 97 OR say about fault?
> Fault is presumed. The debtor must prove that no fault lies with him.

> [!qard]- 7. Apply: a customer receives software built to specification, does not test it, and complains about an obvious defect four months later. What rights are left?
> None for that defect. Without timely inspection and notice the work counts as tacitly approved (Art. 370 OR). Liability remains only for hidden defects and for defects the contractor concealed on purpose.

> [!qard]- 8. Apply: a doctor's treatment does not cure the patient, and a builder's roof leaks. Why does only the builder answer for the result as such?
> The builder has a contract for work and owes a result free of defects. The doctor has a mandate and owes careful activity, not success. He is liable only if he lacked care.
