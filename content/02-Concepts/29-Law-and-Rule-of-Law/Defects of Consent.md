> [!bluf]
> A defect of consent exists when a party's will was formed or declared wrongly. The law knows four: **error**, **deceit**, **duress** and **unfair advantage**.
>
> The contract is not void. It is **non-binding for the affected party**, who must declare this **within one year**. Otherwise the contract counts as approved.
>
> For error the key word is **fundamental** (*wesentlich*). A mistake about one's mere motive does not count. A mistake about a fact that both sides could regard as a necessary basis of the contract does: this is the **fundamental error about the basis** (*Grundlagenirrtum*).

## Definition

| Defect | Norm | Core |
|---|---|---|
| **Error** (*Irrtum*) | Art. 23 ff. OR | A party had a wrong idea when concluding |
| **Deceit** (*Täuschung*) | Art. 28 OR | The other side intentionally caused the wrong idea |
| **Duress** (*Drohung*) | Art. 29 f. OR | A party concluded out of well-founded fear |
| **Unfair advantage** (*Übervorteilung*) | Art. 21 OR | Obvious imbalance, obtained by exploiting distress, inexperience or thoughtlessness |

**Effect of error (Art. 23 OR):** the contract is not binding on the party who was in a **fundamental** error when concluding it.

## Key Points

### When is an error fundamental? (Art. 24 OR)

| Case (Art. 24 Abs. 1) | The party … |
|---|---|
| Ziff. 1 | wanted a **different contract** from the one agreed to |
| Ziff. 2 | had a **different thing** or, where the person mattered, a **different person** in mind |
| Ziff. 3 | promised **considerably more**, or accepted **considerably less**, than intended |
| Ziff. 4 | was mistaken about a **fact** regarded in good faith as a **necessary basis** of the contract |

- **Error of motive is not fundamental** (Art. 24 Abs. 2 OR). Why someone wanted the contract is their own affair.
- **Calculation mistakes** do not make the contract non-binding. They are to be corrected (Art. 24 Abs. 3 OR).

### Fundamental error about the basis (Art. 24 Abs. 1 Ziff. 4 OR)

This is a *qualified* error of motive. Two conditions:

- **Subjective:** the fact was, for the mistaken party, a necessary basis for concluding. Without the error they would not have concluded the contract.
- **Objective:** the party was entitled in good faith to regard the fact as a necessary basis. A reasonable person in business dealings would see it the same way.

Typical example: a painting sold and bought as an original turns out to be a copy.

### Invoking the defect

- **One-year period** (Art. 31 OR). The affected party must tell the other within one year that they do not hold to the contract, or claim back what was performed. Otherwise the contract counts as **approved**.
- **Start of the period:** for error and deceit with the **discovery**, for duress when the fear has **ended**.
- **Negligent error** (Art. 26 OR). Whoever invokes an error caused by their own negligence owes compensation for the damage that the falling away of the contract causes, unless the other side knew or should have known of the error.
- **Burden of proof.** Whoever relies on the error must prove its conditions (Art. 8 ZGB).

## Application

**Checking an error:**
1. What exactly was the wrong idea, and at what moment?
2. Is it one of the cases of Art. 24 Abs. 1 Ziff. 1–3 (error in the declaration)?
3. If not: is it a fundamental error about the basis (Ziff. 4)? Check the subjective and the objective condition.
4. Or is it a mere error of motive (Art. 24 Abs. 2)? Then the contract binds.
5. Was the defect invoked within one year of discovery (Art. 31)?
6. Was the error negligent (Art. 26)? Then compensation is owed.

**Deceit is easier to invoke than error.** With intentional deceit the contract is non-binding even if the error caused is not fundamental (Art. 28 OR).

> [!counter] Critiques & Limits
> - **The line between motive and basis is thin.** Whether a fact was a "necessary basis" is decided case by case and is hard to predict.
> - **Protection of trust.** Every error rule lets one party escape a contract the other relied on. Art. 26 OR softens this with compensation, but only where the mistaken party was negligent.
> - **Overlap with warranty for defects.** A buyer who received a defective thing may have both the rights for defects and the error rules. The short periods of warranty law can thereby be sidestepped, which is criticised.
> - **Art. 21 OR is rarely successful.** The imbalance must be obvious and the weakness must have been exploited.

## Self-Test: Defects of Consent

> [!qard]- 1. Name the four defects of consent with their articles.
> Error (Art. 23 ff. OR), deceit (Art. 28), duress (Art. 29 f.), unfair advantage (Art. 21).

> [!qard]- 2. What is the legal effect of a fundamental error?
> The contract is not binding on the mistaken party (Art. 23 OR). It is not void.

> [!qard]- 3. What are the two conditions of a fundamental error about the basis?
> Subjective: the fact was a necessary basis for the mistaken party, who would not have concluded otherwise. Objective: the party could regard it as a necessary basis in good faith.

> [!qard]- 4. Why is a mere error of motive not enough?
> The motive lies in the party's own sphere. The other side cannot know it and must be able to rely on the contract (Art. 24 Abs. 2 OR).

> [!qard]- 5. Within what time must the defect be invoked, and from when?
> Within one year (Art. 31 OR): from discovery for error and deceit, from the end of the fear for duress.

> [!qard]- 6. What follows if the error was caused by one's own negligence?
> The mistaken party can still invoke it but owes compensation for the resulting damage, unless the other side knew or should have known of the error (Art. 26 OR).

> [!qard]- 7. Apply: a painting sold and bought as an original turns out to be a copy. The seller did not know. Which defect is it, and what must the buyer do?
> A fundamental error about the basis (Art. 24 Abs. 1 Ziff. 4 OR): authenticity was necessary for the buyer, and in good faith a basis of the contract. The buyer must declare the contract non-binding within one year of the discovery (Art. 31 OR).

> [!qard]- 8. Compare: error and deceit. Which is easier to invoke, and why?
> Deceit (Art. 28 OR). With intentional deceit the contract is non-binding even if the error it caused is not fundamental. An error alone counts only if it is fundamental (Art. 24 OR).
