> [!bluf]
> A contract that has been formed by offer and acceptance binds only if it is also **valid**. Five conditions must be met:
> 1. the parties have **capacity to act**,
> 2. the performance is **not impossible**,
> 3. the content stays **within the limits of the law and morality**,
> 4. any **prescribed form** is observed,
> 5. there are **no defects of consent**.
>
> The consequences differ. Failing conditions 2 to 4 makes the contract **void** from the start. A defect of consent makes it **non-binding for one party only**, and only if that party says so in time.

## Definition

| # | Condition | Norm | If it fails |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Capacity to act** (*Handlungsfähigkeit*) | Art. 12–16 ZGB | No valid declaration of intent |
| 2 | **Performance possible** | Art. 20 OR | Void |
| 3 | **Lawful and moral content** | Art. 19–20 OR | Void |
| 4 | **Form** observed where prescribed | Art. 11 OR | Invalid |
| 5 | **No defect of consent** | Art. 21, 23–31 OR | Non-binding for the affected party, see [[Defects of Consent]] |

## Key Points

### 1. Capacity to act

- **Capacity to act** is the ability to create rights and duties through one's own acts (Art. 12 ZGB).
- It needs two things (Art. 13 ZGB): being **of age**, which means 18 completed years (Art. 14 ZGB), and **capacity of judgement**.
- **Minors with capacity of judgement** are not simply unable to act. They can take on obligations with the **consent of their legal representative** (Art. 19 ZGB). Without it the contract stays in suspense until it is approved or refused.
- **Capacity of judgement** (*Urteilsfähigkeit*, Art. 16 ZGB) is the ability to act reasonably. It is presumed and is missing only for specific reasons such as young age, mental disorder or intoxication. It is judged for the concrete transaction at the concrete moment.

### 2 and 3. Possible, lawful, moral

- A contract with an **impossible** or **unlawful** content, or one that offends **morality** (*gute Sitten*), is **void** (Art. 20 Abs. 1 OR).
- **Partial nullity** (Art. 20 Abs. 2 OR): if the defect concerns only parts of the contract, only those parts are void, unless the contract would not have been concluded at all without them.

### 4. Form

- **Rule: freedom of form.** A contract needs a special form only where the law prescribes one (Art. 11 Abs. 1 OR). The parties may also agree on a form themselves (Art. 16 OR).
- **If a prescribed form is not observed**, the contract is invalid, unless the law says otherwise (Art. 11 Abs. 2 OR).
- **Why the law makes exceptions:**

    | Purpose | Examples |
    |---|---|
    | **Protection against haste** | Guarantee (*Bürgschaft*, Art. 493 OR), sale of land (Art. 216 OR), promise of a gift (Art. 243 OR) |
    | **Publicity and legal certainty** | Amendment of the articles of a company limited by shares (Art. 647 OR), assignment of a claim (Art. 165 OR) |
    | **Information** | Notice of the right of revocation (Art. 40d OR), notice of a rent increase (Art. 269d OR) |
    | **Securing evidence** | Landlord's consent to alterations by the tenant (Art. 260a OR) |

### 5. Defects of consent

Error, deceit, duress and unfair advantage. They do not make the contract void. They give the affected party the choice to hold to the contract or not. See [[Defects of Consent]].

## Application

- **Order of checks.** First formation ([[Formation of a Contract]]), then the five validity conditions, then content and performance. Stop at the first condition that fails.
- **Void versus non-binding.** Void means the contract never had effect, for anyone, and a court takes notice of it on its own. Non-binding means only the protected party can invoke the defect, and loses that right by waiting.
- **After a contract falls away,** what was already handed over is claimed back through unjust enrichment or as property (see [[Obligation (OR)]]).

> [!counter] Critiques & Limits
> - **Morality is an open standard.** What offends *gute Sitten* changes with society (see [[Values and Norms]]), so courts decide it case by case.
> - **Form protects and traps.** A form rule meant to protect one party can be used by the other to escape a contract that both wanted. Courts limit this with the ban on abuse of rights (Art. 2 ZGB).
> - **Capacity of judgement is hard to prove afterwards.** It is presumed, so the party who denies it carries the burden.

## Self-Test: Contract Validity

> [!qard]- 1. Name the five validity conditions.
> Capacity to act, performance not impossible, content within the limits of law and morality, prescribed form observed, no defects of consent.

> [!qard]- 2. Who has capacity to act?
> Whoever is of age (18) and has capacity of judgement (Art. 13, 14, 16 ZGB).

> [!qard]- 3. What is the consequence of an unlawful content?
> The contract is void (Art. 20 Abs. 1 OR). If only a part is affected, only that part is void, unless the contract would not have been concluded without it.

> [!qard]- 4. Name the four purposes of form requirements, each with an example.
> Protection against haste (guarantee), publicity and legal certainty (change of company articles), information (notice of a rent increase), securing evidence (landlord's consent to alterations).

> [!qard]- 5. What is the difference between void and non-binding?
> Void: without effect from the start, for everyone. Non-binding: only the protected party can refuse to be bound, and must do so in time.

> [!qard]- 6. Apply: a 17-year-old signs a lease, and an adult promises a guarantee orally. What is the problem in each case?
> The minor lacks full capacity to act, since being of age means 18 completed years (Art. 14 ZGB). The lease binds only with the consent of the legal representative (Art. 19 ZGB). The guarantee does not observe the prescribed form (Art. 493 OR) and is invalid.

> [!qard]- 7. Apply: one clause of a contract is unlawful. Is the whole contract void?
> Only that part is void (partial nullity, Art. 20 Abs. 2 OR), unless the contract would not have been concluded at all without it.
