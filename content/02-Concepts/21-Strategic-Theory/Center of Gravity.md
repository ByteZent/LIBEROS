> [!bluf]
> The center of gravity (CoG) is the element of an adversary's (or one's own) system on which its overall power and cohesion depend. Striking it produces disproportionate effects. The concept comes from [[Carl von Clausewitz|Clausewitz]] and is central to NATO and US operational planning, but it is **contested**: doctrine treats it as a *source of strength*, while Clausewitz meant a *focal point of cohesion*. That difference changes what planners target.

## Definition

> [!source] Clausewitz, On War, Book VIII, ch. 4
> "…the hub of all power and movement, on which everything depends. That is the point against which all our energies should be directed."

**US Joint doctrine (JP 5-0):** the source of power that provides moral or physical strength, freedom of action, or will to act.

**In my words:** the thing that, if it fails, makes the whole opposing effort come apart. It is not simply "their strongest unit".

## Key Points

- Clausewitz drew on mechanics: a CoG exists only where a system has **cohesion**. A loose coalition may have several CoGs, or none.
- Examples he gives: the enemy's army, the capital, the principal ally, the leader, public opinion.
- **Strange's CG–CC–CR–CV model** (1996) made the concept usable for planners: *Critical Capabilities* → *Critical Requirements* → *Critical Vulnerabilities*. You attack the CoG *indirectly* through its vulnerabilities.
- The CoG exists at every level (strategic, operational, tactical) and can **shift** over the course of a conflict.

## Origins & Evolution

1. Clausewitz, *On War*, Books VI and VIII: the concept is borrowed from physics.
2. Rediscovered by the US military in the 1980s (FM 100-5, 1986), in the wake of the post-Vietnam Clausewitz revival.
3. Joe Strange (1996) supplied the CC/CR/CV method. Warden's "Five Rings" (1995) offered an airpower-centric, systems alternative.
4. Echevarria (2002) criticised the doctrinal drift from "focal point" to "source of strength".

## Application

- **Operational design:** identify friendly and adversary CoGs at each level, then derive decisive points and lines of operation. See [[Ends-Ways-Means]].
- Illustrative debate: was Ukraine's strategic CoG in 2022 its army, its political leadership, or Western support? The answer changes where resources go.

> [!counter] Critiques & Limits
> - **Ambiguity:** planners routinely disagree on what the CoG is, so the analysis can end up justifying preferences.
> - **Echevarria:** doctrine's "source of strength" reading loses Clausewitz's insight about *cohesion*.
> - **Networked adversaries** (insurgencies, hybrid actors) may lack the cohesion the concept assumes.
