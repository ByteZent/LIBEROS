> [!bluf]
> Deterrence means persuading an adversary *not to act* by convincing them that the costs or risks of acting outweigh the benefits. It works either by **denial** (you won't succeed) or by **punishment** (you'll pay too high a price). It depends on capability, credibility and communication, and ultimately on the adversary's *perception*, which the deterrer can shape but not control.

## Definition

**Snyder (1961):** deterrence works by threatening to impose costs or to deny gains. He distinguished it from *defence*, which reduces costs if deterrence fails.

**In my words:** deterrence is a relationship inside the adversary's head. My weapons matter only insofar as they change what the adversary expects to happen.

## Key Points

- **Denial vs punishment** (Snyder): make the objective unattainable, or make achieving it unbearably costly.
- **Three Cs:** capability × credibility × communication. If any one is zero, deterrence fails.
- **General vs immediate** (Morgan, 1977): standing deterrence of latent threats vs deterrence during an acute crisis.
- **Deterrence vs compellence** (Schelling, 1966): deterrence prevents an action; compellence forces one. Compellence is generally harder.
- **Extended deterrence:** protecting allies (e.g. NATO Art. 5, the nuclear umbrella). Credibility is the chronic problem: "Would Washington trade Boston for Hamburg?"

## Origins & Evolution

The idea is ancient. The formal theory developed in the nuclear age (Brodie, Schelling, Snyder, Kahn). After 1991 it shifted towards "tailored" deterrence of rogue states and terrorists, and since 2014 it has returned to great-power competition, with renewed attention to conventional deterrence and deterrence of hybrid and cyber threats.

## Application

- NATO's eastern flank: the shift from *tripwire* forward presence (punishment logic) towards forward defence and denial after 2022.

> [!counter] Critiques & Limits
> - **Rationality assumption:** deterrence presumes a cost-benefit calculus that leaders under domestic pressure or misperception may not perform (Jervis, Lebow & Stein).
> - **Unfalsifiability:** successful deterrence is a non-event, so it is hard to prove it worked.
> - **Grey-zone activity** is calibrated to stay below deterrence thresholds.
