> [!bluf]
> German has one word, *Politik*. English has three, and political science uses them to take a political event apart:
> - **Polity** is the **form**: institutions and rules of the game.
> - **Policy** is the **content**: what is decided in a policy field.
> - **Politics** is the **process**: how the decision comes about and who takes part, through debate, conflict and negotiation.
>
> They are not three kinds of things. They are **three facets of the same thing**. Which one stands in front depends on the question you ask.

## Definition

Politics is **social action aimed at decisions and steering mechanisms that are generally binding and regulate how people live together** [@bernauer2025einfuhrung, p. 29].

The definition has two parts:

- **Social action** (*soziales Handeln*): a person acts in direct connection with other people, so social action is always interactive [@bernauer2025einfuhrung, pp. 29–30]. Weber's own wording is action oriented towards the behaviour of others [@weber1922wirtschaft, § 1]. "Social" carries no value judgement: planning a concert visit with friends is social, and so is organising a violent demonstration. Running from a volcano or opening an umbrella is not.
- **Generally binding** (*allgemein verbindlich*): all members of a certain group have to accept and follow the result. The group can be the residents of a street, the population of a state or the whole world. Steering mechanisms are laws, administrative procedures, declarations, informal agreements, unwritten norms and customs, and the organisations that carry them. They can settle a single case (sending troops abroad) or hold permanently (human rights law). The permanent ones are called **political institutions** [@bernauer2025einfuhrung, pp. 30–31].

Two things do **not** matter for the definition [@bernauer2025einfuhrung, p. 31]:

- **Success.** A failed attempt to create a binding rule is still politics, for example the efforts to abolish the death penalty in the USA.
- **Legality and morality.** The attacks of 11 September 2001 were illegal and morally indefensible, and Al-Qaida still acted politically.

In my own words: an action becomes political when it tries to shape rules that everybody in a group has to follow. All political action is social action. Not all social action is political: helping a neighbour move is social, but it binds nobody else.

## Key Points

| | Polity | Policy | Politics |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Facet** | Form, structure | Content | Process |
| **Question** | In which structures does it happen? | What is decided? | How does it come about? |
| **Covers** | The institutions and organisations in which political action takes place: the frame of order and action. Also the structural make-up of a system | The substance of a policy field, and the tasks, goals and design of programmes, strategies and measures | The processes and the actors in them: will formation, decision, implementation |
| **Examples** | Parliament, government, courts, the written constitution; democracy or autocracy; proportional or majority voting | Environmental policy, foreign policy, family policy | Public debate, demonstrations, legislative procedure, administrative orders, monitoring and sanctions, coalition building, treaty negotiations, also crises and wars between states |

The rows *Covers* and *Examples* follow the textbook [@bernauer2025einfuhrung, pp. 41–42]. "Rules of the game" as a short name for polity comes from the lecture. The three points below are how the lecture used the triad. The book itself only says that political phenomena can be assigned to the three categories.

- **One event, three readings.** A debate in the Bundeshaus is a debate (politics), about a concrete bill (policy), inside parliament and its rules (polity).
- **The question picks the facet.** "Why did the bill pass?" is a politics question. "Does the bill work?" is a policy question. "Would it have passed under a different electoral system?" is a polity question.
- **The facets shape each other.** Polity sets who may decide and how. That steers politics. Politics produces policy. And some policies change the polity, for example a constitutional amendment.

## Origins & Evolution

- The three English words are everyday language. Political science uses them as three partial aspects (*Teilaspekte*) of the one German word *Politik*, because the English terms capture them more precisely [@bernauer2025einfuhrung, p. 41].
- The textbook ties permanent steering mechanisms to North's definition of institutions: constraints made by people that structure political, economic and social interaction, both informal (taboos, customs, traditions) and formal (constitutions, laws, property rights) [@bernauer2025einfuhrung, p. 30].
- The definition of politics through **binding decisions** stands in a line with Easton, who defined politics as the "authoritative allocation of values for a society" [@easton1965systems]. This link is mine, not the textbook's.

## Application

**Switzerland and Germany differ in polity.** Germany has a Federal Constitutional Court in Karlsruhe that can strike down federal laws. Switzerland has none: the Federal Supreme Court must apply federal acts even if they conflict with the constitution (Art. 190 BV). The same policy conflict therefore runs through different processes in the two countries.

**The boundaries are not sharp.** The Swiss consultation procedure (*Vernehmlassung*) is a fixed institution (polity). It is also the phase in which cantons, parties and associations voice their demands on a draft law (politics).

**A security example.** The purchase of new fighter aircraft touches all three:
- **Polity:** the militia army and its tasks in the constitution [@bv1999art58], parliament's power over the budget, the referendum.
- **Policy:** which aircraft, how many, for which tasks, at what cost.
- **Politics:** the parliamentary debate, the campaign, and the popular vote of September 2020, won by a very narrow margin.

> [!counter] Critiques & Limits
> - **A sorting tool, not a theory.** The triad classifies. It explains nothing and predicts nothing.
> - **Real cases are mixed.** Most items belong to more than one facet, as the *Vernehmlassung* shows. Forcing them into one box can hide exactly what is interesting.
> - **State-centred examples.** The definition of politics is broad: it covers binding rules made by intermediary actors too, such as certification and label systems of industry associations and civil society for global supply chains [@bernauer2025einfuhrung, pp. 31–32]. The examples for polity are still parliament, government and courts. Outside the state, "polity" is much harder to locate.
> - **What counts as political is itself contested.** The definition draws a line between social and political action. The textbook notes that the traditional split between a private sphere (private life, markets) and a public one (politics, the state) is blurring. Where the line runs (family, economy, religion) is one of the oldest political conflicts.

## Self-Test: Polity-Policy-Politics

> [!qard]- 1. Define politics in one sentence.
> Social action aimed at decisions and steering mechanisms that are generally binding and regulate how people live together.

> [!qard]- 2. Why is all political action social action, but not the other way round?
> Political action is always directed at other people, so it is social. But social action only becomes political when it aims at rules that bind a whole group. Helping a neighbour is social and binds nobody.

> [!qard]- 3. Sort: the Federal Constitution, the CO₂ Act, a referendum campaign.
> Polity (it organises the institutions), policy (content of climate policy), politics (a process of will formation and decision).

> [!qard]- 4. Why can the same event be sorted into all three?
> Because the three terms describe facets, not separate objects. A debate in parliament is a process, about a content, inside a structure. The research question decides which facet is in front.

> [!qard]- 5. Give one example where polity and politics are hard to separate.
> The Swiss *Vernehmlassung*: a fixed institution and at the same time a phase of the decision process.

> [!qard]- 6. Apply: sort three questions about the fighter aircraft purchase. Which aircraft, and how many? Why was the vote so close? Why could the people vote at all?
> Policy: the content. Politics: the process of campaign and debate. Polity: the referendum as an institution.

> [!qard]- 7. Explain: how do the three facets shape each other?
> Polity sets who may decide and how, which steers politics. Politics produces policy. Some policies change the polity in turn, for example a constitutional amendment.

> [!qard]- 8. Is a failed or illegal attempt to impose a rule still politics?
> Yes. The definition asks what the action aims at, not whether it succeeds, is legal or is morally defensible. The textbook's examples: failed efforts to abolish the US death penalty, and the attacks of 11 September 2001.
