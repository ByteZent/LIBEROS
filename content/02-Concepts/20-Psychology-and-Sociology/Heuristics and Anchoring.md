> [!bluf]
> A **heuristic** is a mental rule of thumb for reaching a judgement quickly. Because the mind has limited capacity, heuristics are necessary and usually work. Because they simplify, they also produce **systematic errors**, most of all under stress.
>
> Two cases matter here. A **stereotype** is stored knowledge about a group that steers what we notice, and so confirms itself. The **anchoring effect** is the pull of a number given beforehand on any estimate made afterwards. In a negotiation this means: **whoever makes the first offer sets the anchor**, and the result ends up closer to it.

## Definition

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Heuristic** | A cognitive rule of thumb that people use to reach a judgement. Often gives good results. Because it simplifies, it can also lead to errors |
| **Social stereotype** | A cognitive structure containing our knowledge, beliefs and expectations about a social group |
| **Anchoring effect** | People are unconsciously influenced in estimates and decisions by numerical information in their surroundings. A numerical judgement is pulled in the direction of an anchor set beforehand |

## Key Points

### Stereotypes

- Stereotypes are very **resistant to change**.
- The reason lies in perception: when we observe a member of a group, we take in information that **fits** the stereotype more readily than information that **contradicts** it. The stereotype selects its own evidence.

### Anchoring

- **Property valuation.** Laypeople and estate agents were asked to value a house. All received the same information, except the owner's asking price (low or high). The higher the asking price, the higher the estimated value, for the experts too [@northcraft1987experts].
- **Arbitrary anchors work as well.** Asked whether Japan has more or fewer than 100 islands, people then guessed about 190 on average. Asked "more or fewer than 1,000?", they guessed about 1,370.
- **Anchoring in negotiations:**
    - The outcome correlates positively with the **first offer** [@galinsky2001first]. The side that opens has the advantage.
    - The higher a seller's first offer, the higher the agreed price. The lower a buyer's first offer, the lower the price.
    - A **precise** first offer (40.50 instead of 40) is a stronger anchor than a round one. The other side concedes more.

### Why heuristics exist

- The cognitive system has **limited resources** (see [[Attention and Automatisation]]).
- Heuristics are functional in everyday decisions.
- They become dangerous **under stress**, when there is no capacity left to check the quick judgement.

## Origins & Evolution

- The research programme on heuristics and biases, including anchoring, goes back to Tversky and Kahneman [@tversky1974judgment].
- Kahneman later described two modes of thinking: a fast, automatic one that runs on heuristics and a slow, effortful one that can check it [@kahneman2011thinking].

## Application

- **Negotiating.**
    - If you know the range of sensible outcomes, **open first** and open ambitiously but defensibly.
    - Give a **precise** figure. It signals that you have calculated.
    - If the other side opens, do not adjust from their number. Name the anchor for what it is and counter from your own prepared figure.
    - Prepare your own value before the talk. An anchor is strongest on someone who has no number of their own.
- **Estimates in planning.** The first figure mentioned in a staff meeting (time needed, enemy strength, casualties) anchors everything after it. Have people write down their own estimate **before** the discussion, and let the most senior speak last.
- **Decision under time pressure.** Experienced leaders decide by recognising a pattern, which is a heuristic and a good one. The risk is the situation that only looks familiar. A short check ("what would have to be true for this to be something else?") costs seconds.
- **Assessing people.** A stereotype about a group (a language region, an arm of service, a generation) makes a leader notice what fits and overlook what does not. First impressions and the previous rating act as anchors on the next rating.
- **Intelligence.** The first assessment of an opponent anchors later ones. New evidence is adjusted to it too little. This is how an estimate survives long after the facts have changed.

> [!counter] Critiques & Limits
> - **Heuristics are not just errors.** Another line of research (fast and frugal heuristics) shows that simple rules often beat complex calculation in uncertain, real-world settings. The laboratory tasks are built to make heuristics fail.
> - **Anchoring is robust, but its size varies.** Knowledge, motivation and warning reduce it without removing it. Extreme anchors can backfire by ending the negotiation.
> - **Knowing about a bias does not remove it.** Like a visual illusion, it persists. Procedures help more than awareness.
> - **Stereotypes contain information.** They are generalisations that are sometimes accurate on average. The error lies in applying the average to the individual and in not updating.
