> [!bluf]
> Perception is not a camera. It is the **interpretation** of information from the environment and from inside the body: an **active (re)construction**. What we perceive depends on two things: the **stimulus** that reaches the senses, and the **knowledge** already stored in memory (experience, interests, values, prejudices, abilities, feelings and needs).
>
> So people do not react to the world as it objectively is. They react to the world **as it appears to them**. Two soldiers in the same situation perceive two different situations. That is the starting point of every briefing, every report and every deception.

## Definition

**Perception:** the process of interpreting information from the environment and the inside of the body.

The intake and interpretation of sensory signals is influenced by
1. the **stimulus energy** taken up by the sensory systems and the brain, and
2. the **knowledge** stored in memory before the experience:
    - previous experience,
    - interests, values, attitudes and prejudices,
    - abilities and skills,
    - feelings, moods and needs.

**Cognition** (Latin *cognoscere*, to recognise) in the wide sense means **information processing**. Cognitive psychology explains behaviour at the level of mental representations and processes. Its fields are perception, attention, memory, and thinking, deciding and problem-solving.

## Key Points

### The perceptual process

| Step | What happens                                                              | Example             |
| ---- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------- |
| 1    | **Environmental stimulus**                                                | A drone in the air  |
| 2    | Light is reflected and transformed (sound is carried)                     |                     |
| 3    | **Transduction** in the receptors: physical energy becomes a nerve signal |                     |
| 4    | **Neural processing**                                                     |                     |
| 5    | **Perception (Wahrnehmung)**                                             | "I hear something." |
| 6    | **Recognising (Erkennen)**                                                | "It is a drone."    |
| 7    | **Acting (Handeln)**                                                      | "I take cover."     |

Steps 5 to 7 are where knowledge enters. A soldier who has never heard that drone hears a noise. A trained one hears a threat.

### What shapes perception from the inside

Expectations, the emotional system, values and prejudices, memory and motivation all filter and colour what is taken in. This is why cognitive psychology matters for **cognitive warfare**: whoever shapes expectations shapes what is perceived.

### Illusions

Our senses can present a stimulus pattern in a way that is demonstrably wrong, and knowing this does not make the illusion go away.

| Illusion | What is misjudged |
|---|---|
| **Müller-Lyer** | The length of two lines with arrowheads pointing in or out |
| **Zöllner** | Whether lines are parallel |
| **Ebbinghaus** | The size of a circle surrounded by larger or smaller circles |

### Basic assumptions of cognitive psychology

- Perception, thinking, behaviour and experience are results of cognitive processes.
- Behaviour is governed by complex plans to reach goals.
- The human is not only a processing and reacting organism but is **active** in knowing and acting.

## Application

- **Reports are perceptions.** A contact report says what the observer recognised, not what was there. Good reporting formats separate observation ("I see three vehicles") from interpretation ("probably a reconnaissance element").
- **Training builds the knowledge that recognition needs.** Recognition training (vehicles, aircraft, sounds) shortens the step from perceiving to recognising.
- **Expectation makes blind and makes see.** A sentry who expects an attack recognises threats faster, and also sees threats that are not there. This is the route to errors such as firing on one's own troops.
- **Camouflage and deception** work on steps 5 and 6: let the enemy receive the stimulus but fail to recognise it, or recognise something else.
- **Leadership.** Before judging a subordinate's reaction, ask how the situation looked to them (see [[Attribution]]).

> [!counter] Critiques & Limits
> - **The step model is a simplification.** Processing is not a one-way line. Knowledge and expectation act back on the early steps.
> - **Laboratory illusions are artificial.** They show that perception constructs, but in ordinary conditions perception is remarkably accurate. The lesson is "know when it fails", not "distrust your eyes".
> - **"Everyone has their own reality" can be overdrawn.** People in the same situation mostly agree on what they see. The differences lie in what they attend to and how they evaluate it.

## Self-Test: Perception

> [!qard]- 1. Define perception.
> The process of interpreting information from the environment and from inside the body.

> [!qard]- 2. Which two things influence the intake and interpretation of sensory signals?
> The stimulus energy taken up by the senses and brain, and the knowledge stored in memory before the experience.

> [!qard]- 3. Name the last three steps of the perceptual process with an example.
> Perceiving ("I hear something"), recognising ("it is a drone"), acting ("I take cover").

> [!qard]- 4. What kinds of prior knowledge shape perception?
> Experience; interests, values, attitudes and prejudices; abilities and skills; feelings, moods and needs.

> [!qard]- 5. Why is perception called an active (re)construction?
> Because the brain does not copy the stimulus. It interprets it using stored knowledge, which is why illusions occur and why people perceive the same situation differently.

> [!qard]- 6. Apply: a sentry reports "enemy reconnaissance element approaching". What is wrong with the report, and how should it read?
> It states the interpretation as if it were the observation. Separate them: "I see three vehicles moving north" (observation), "probably a reconnaissance element" (interpretation).

> [!qard]- 7. Explain: on which steps of the perceptual process do camouflage and recognition training work?
> Both on perceiving and recognising. Camouflage lets the enemy receive the stimulus but not recognise it, or recognise something else. Training builds the knowledge that shortens the step from perceiving to recognising.
