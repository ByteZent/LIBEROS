> [!bluf]
> Sociology studies **society**: how people live together, how that order holds, and how it changes. It needs **critical distance** from the events it looks at.
>
> Any sociological question can be placed on three axes: the **level** (from single social action up to whole society), the **basic process** (for example power, conflict, socialisation) and the **field** (politics, economy, military, family). The main schools differ in what they think drives social life: **meaning**, **function** or **conflict**, and in how far they see people as shaped by culture or by biology.
>
> **Military sociology** applies all this to the armed forces as an organisation inside a changing society.

## Definition

- **Object:** society, or human living together.
- **Aim:** to understand society, social processes and social change: causes, courses, trends.
- **Condition:** critical distance from social events and appearances.
- **Name:** from Latin *socius* (companion, community) and Greek *logos* (teaching). Coined by Auguste Comte (1798–1857).

### Basic questions

- How does the individual relate to society, the state, institutions and organisations?
- How are complex societies held together? What integrates them?
- How are values and norms passed on?
- Why is there power, and which forms of it count as legitimate?

## Key Points

### Three dimensions of sociology

| Axis | Values |
|---|---|
| **Level of aggregation** (micro to macro) | Social action → role → group → organisation → institution → society |
| **Basic processes** | Interaction, communication, exchange, cooperation, power and influence, conflict, social control, socialisation, values and norms, stratification |
| **Field** | Politics, economy, education, **military**, family, medicine, youth, science, religion |

A study is located by one value on each axis. Example: *socialisation* in the *group* in the *military* is the question of how a recruit platoon turns civilians into soldiers.

### Schools

| School | Core idea | Image of the human |
|---|---|---|
| **Symbolic interactionism** | People act on the basis of the meaning things have for them. Meaning arises in interaction | A seeker of meaning |
| **Functionalism** | Every part of society fulfils a function for the whole (for example the family for society) | A role-bearer |
| **Conflict theories** | Conflict is what creates and changes structures | An interested party |
| **Sociobiology, evolutionary psychology** | Social behaviour (sex relations, aggression, dominance) has a strong genetic base | Largely fixed by nature |

- **Classical sociology** (the first three) sees the human as a **cultural being**, formed by society. Its tendency is optimistic: society can be changed for the better.
- **Sociobiology** sees social behaviour as strongly **genetically fixed**. Its tendency is pessimistic: people are as they are. The danger is that this is used to justify inequality politically (social Darwinism).

### Origins

Sociology arose from the upheavals of the 18th and 19th centuries:
- the Enlightenment: away from absolutism and feudalism, towards democracy and human and civil rights,
- the rise of the bourgeoisie,
- the French Revolution.

When the old order was no longer self-evident, society itself became a question.

## Application

- **Military sociology** separates military and society analytically and studies the armed forces as an organisation within social and value change. Typical topics: demography, the structure of society, organisational sociology, and the peculiarities of the military as an organisation.
- **Choosing the level.** The same problem looks different on each level. Low motivation in a company can be read as individual action, as a group norm, as a feature of the organisation, or as a value shift in society. Each reading suggests a different remedy.
- **Choosing the school.** A functionalist asks what a ritual does for cohesion. A conflict theorist asks whom it serves and whom it keeps down. An interactionist asks what it means to those who take part. All three are worth asking before a commander bans or keeps a tradition.

> [!counter] Critiques & Limits
> - **Schools are not a menu of equals.** Most current research combines them or works with narrower theories. The four-box picture is a teaching aid.
> - **Nature versus nurture is a false alternative.** Behaviour comes from the interplay of both. Neither pure position is held seriously today.
> - **Critical distance has limits.** Sociologists are members of the society they study, and military sociologists are often employed by the forces they study.
> - **Micro and macro are hard to connect.** Explaining how single actions add up to social structures, and how structures shape actions, is an unsolved core problem.

## Self-Test: Sociology

> [!qard]- 1. What is the object and the aim of sociology?
> Object: society, human living together. Aim: understanding society, social processes and social change.

> [!qard]- 2. Name the levels from micro to macro.
> Social action, role, group, organisation, institution, society.

> [!qard]- 3. Name the three axes on which a sociological question is placed.
> Level of aggregation, basic process, field.

> [!qard]- 4. How do classical sociology and sociobiology differ in their image of the human?
> Classical sociology: a cultural being formed by society, change for the better is possible. Sociobiology: social behaviour strongly fixed by genes, people are as they are.

> [!qard]- 5. From which historical upheavals did sociology arise?
> The Enlightenment, the rise of the bourgeoisie and the French Revolution.

> [!qard]- 6. Apply: a commander wants to ban an initiation ritual. What would a functionalist, a conflict theorist and an interactionist each ask first?
> Functionalist: what does it do for cohesion? Conflict theorist: whom does it serve, and whom does it keep down? Interactionist: what does it mean to those who take part?

> [!qard]- 7. Apply: motivation in a company is low. Read it on four levels, and say why the choice of level matters.
> As individual action, as a group norm, as a feature of the organisation, or as a value shift in society. Each reading points to a different remedy, so the level chosen decides the measure.
