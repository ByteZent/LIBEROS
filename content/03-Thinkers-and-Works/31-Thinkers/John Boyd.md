> [!bluf]
> US Air Force fighter pilot and strategist (1927–1997) who never wrote a book, yet reshaped Western military thinking through briefings. He is indispensable for the [[OODA Loop]] and for the theory of **manoeuvre warfare**: the idea that conflict is won by out-adapting the opponent and breaking his cohesion, not by out-producing and destroying him.

## Context

- Korean War fighter pilot. Afterwards an instructor at the USAF Fighter Weapons School, nicknamed **"40-Second Boyd"** for his standing bet that he could beat any opponent from a position of disadvantage within 40 seconds.
- Wrote the *Aerial Attack Study*, the first systematic manual of air-combat tactics.
- Developed **energy-manoeuvrability theory** with mathematician Thomas Christie. Became a leading figure of the "Fighter Mafia" that pushed for the light, agile F-16.
- After retiring as a colonel (1975) he devoted himself to strategy. His work circulated as long briefings, later collected as *A Discourse on Winning and Losing*.

## Core Ideas

- **[[OODA Loop]]:** competition as a contest of adaptation; orientation as the *Schwerpunkt*.
- **Destruction and creation:** every mental model becomes obsolete, so we must keep breaking and rebuilding our concepts.
- **Manoeuvre over attrition:** target the opponent's **cohesion** and orientation, generating surprise, ambiguity and confusion. Compare [[Center of Gravity]].
- **Organic command and control:** *Einheit*, *Fingerspitzengefühl*, *Auftragstaktik*, *Schwerpunkt*: shared orientation instead of centralised control.
- **People, ideas, hardware, in that order:** moral and mental factors matter more than technology.

## Key Works

- *Aerial Attack Study* (1960/1964)
- "Destruction and Creation" (1976)
- "Patterns of Conflict" (1976–86)
- "Organic Design for Command and Control" (1987)
- "The Essence of Winning and Losing" (1995)
- Collected: *A Discourse on Winning and Losing*, ed. Grant T. Hammond (2018)

## Influence

- US Marine Corps doctrine: *FMFM 1 Warfighting* (1989) and its successor MCDP 1.
- Military reform debates of the 1980s; widely cited in business strategy, cyber security and emergency management.

> [!counter] Critiques
> - Boyd's ideas survive mainly as simplified slogans ("be faster"), which he would have rejected.
> - His briefing-based, highly synthetic method draws on science by analogy. Admirers call it deep, critics call it loose.
> - Claims about his direct influence on specific campaigns (e.g. the 1991 Gulf War) are often overstated in popular accounts.
