> [!bluf]
> [[Carl von Clausewitz|Clausewitz]]'s unfinished treatise (8 books, published posthumously in 1832) is a theory of war as a political act shaped by violence, chance and friction. It is less a manual than a way of thinking: it teaches judgement, not rules.

## Argument

War is "an act of force to compel our enemy to do our will" (Book I, ch. 1). Its logic pushes towards extremes, but in reality war is moderated by its **political purpose**, by **friction** and by **uncertainty**. The political object therefore determines the military aim and the effort to be made.

## Reading Guide

- Clausewitz's **note of 1827** states he intended to revise the whole work around two ideas: the *two types of war* (limited / to overthrow the enemy) and war as *continuation of policy*. Only Book I, ch. 1 was fully revised, so read it as his most mature statement.
- Read Book I, then Book VIII, before the rest.

## Contribution & Limits

> [!assessment] My reading
> Its lasting value lies in the *method*: it holds political purpose and war's own dynamics in tension instead of resolving them. Its limits are its Napoleonic evidence base, and the silence on sea power, economics and technology, which later theorists had to add.
